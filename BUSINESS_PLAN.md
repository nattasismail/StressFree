# StressFree — Startup Blueprint (Version 1)

## Mission
Make university life more manageable by helping students organise academic work and build sustainable study habits.

## Initial audience
South African university students who feel overloaded by deadlines, classes, tests and competing responsibilities. Initial focus: students at one or two universities for validation, then expand.

## Positioning
StressFree is an academic organisation tool with lightweight wellbeing reflection—not a clinical mental-health service and not a promise to improve grades.

## MVP
- Task/deadline planner
- Priority field
- Focus timer
- Optional stress check-in
- Local browser data storage

## Differentiation hypotheses to test
- Simple, low-friction interface
- Designed around the academic workload
- Academic planning combined with optional wellbeing reflection
- South African student context and language/semester patterns

## Business model experiments (do not implement payments until validated)
1. Free MVP to gather feedback and establish usage.
2. Student premium: advanced planning, recurring tasks, calendar export/sync, saved routines, and insights. Test pricing only after interviews.
3. Campus partnerships or sponsored access if evidence and interest emerge.
4. Carefully vetted affiliate/partner offers only if relevant and disclosed.

## Validation plan
- Recruit 15–30 students for usability testing.
- Ask them to complete: add a task, set a due date, use focus timer, check-in, find tasks.
- Record task success, confusion, feature requests, and willingness to return.
- Run a 2–4 week pilot. Track repeat use and retention, not only sign-ups.
- Do not claim stress reduction or grade improvement without appropriate evidence.

## Success signals (early hypotheses)
- Most testers complete core tasks without assistance.
- Students return to use the planner during the pilot.
- Feedback identifies a clear, repeated problem the app solves.
- Some users express credible willingness to pay or a campus partner expresses interest.

## 90-day plan
### Weeks 1–2
Review MVP, interview students, refine scope and test usability.
### Weeks 3–4
Add feedback mechanism, improve UI, test mobile, and fix bugs.
### Weeks 5–8
Pilot with a small student group, gather anonymised usage/feedback.
### Weeks 9–12
Review evidence, prioritise features, test monetisation, and decide whether to pursue registration/partnerships.

## Risks
- Competition and weak differentiation.
- Student willingness to pay may be low.
- Sensitive wellbeing notes require privacy safeguards.
- Local-storage MVP can lose data and does not sync across devices.
- Evidence in the source portfolios is correlational; correlation is not causation.
- Growth and revenue are not guaranteed.
